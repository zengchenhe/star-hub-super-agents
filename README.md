# Super-Agents on a Star Hub

## — Organizational Layering and Asynchronous Communication in a Heterogeneous Multi-Agent System: Challenges, Methods, Lessons Learned, and Best Practices

**Keywords**: multi-agent systems; star topology; envelope protocol; asynchronous communication; authorization governance; evidence chains

## A Note on Sources and Reading Conventions

- **Sources of fact**: The factual basis of this paper is not any one party's record. It is a merged document (hereafter "the four-party merged record") compiled from the records each of the four parties kept for itself — the Management–Coordination Agent, the Management–Scheduling Agent, the Middle-layer Steward Agent, and the Cloud Business Execution Agent — merged along a timeline, with points of disagreement listed separately rather than reconciled. Every fact in this paper can be traced back to an entry and a source in that merged record. Where the four parties' accounts disagree, the paper says so plainly ("the records do not agree") and does not take sides. The external comparison at the end of Section 7 has its own sources, noted at each point, and is not mixed with the factual basis.
- **Reading conventions**: This paper is written in plain language. The first time a technical term appears, a plain-language explanation follows immediately.

## Abstract

When one person runs several AI assistants at once, the first bottleneck is usually not that the models are not smart enough. It is two small things: the person's attention does not stretch far enough, and the assistants cannot talk to each other clearly. This paper describes one such system. It has four members: two management-layer agents (the Management–Coordination Agent, which initiates work, coordinates, integrates, and accepts results; and the Management–Scheduling Agent, which handles organization and scheduling; together, the two management seats, facing the system's owner directly); one Middle-layer Steward Agent (which shares the management layer's daily workload and watches over the execution layer); a group of execution-layer members (a set of local automation bots, plus one sandbox-restricted third-party agent, the Cloud Business Execution Agent, which works only on dispatched tasks and never initiates projects of its own); and the owner of the system (hereafter "the Owner").

They share no runtime, no public API, and no shared memory. The only thing all four parties can reach is a single directory on a consumer cloud drive. The solution had to fit that constraint: communication is organized as a **star** (every member connects to one central hub rather than pairwise, so that no pair of members needs its own separate link), and the smallest unit of communication is the **envelope package** (a file package carrying a manifest and fingerprints: an envelope description, attachments, and a hash list; the recipient recomputes each fingerprint and accepts the package only if they all match). "Delivered" is never established by someone saying "I sent it"; it is established only by recomputable evidence.

This paper answers four retrospective questions: in the first ten days of real operation, **what challenges did this system encounter, what methods did it use to solve them, what lessons did it learn, and what best practices did it settle on?** It does two further things. First, it lays the ten days' evolution out as an explicit timeline (see the Timeline Overview and Section 2): almost none of this system's rules were designed in advance and then implemented; most were added after something went wrong or jammed, and the order in which things happened is itself part of the argument. Second, it distills the practice into our own conceptual framework (Section 7): one premise, five core concepts, and one principle running through all of it, every one of them grown out of our own incidents. At the end of that section is a short comparison with an external theory — DeepSeek's Super-agent, the closest match to this system in the published literature — stating plainly where the two agree, where they differ, and what the other side has that we do not. Every example in this paper is a real event, with its time and source given; where the four parties' records disagree, the paper marks it rather than smoothing it over.

## 1 System Overview (Minimum Background for New Readers)

- **The organization has three layers**: the first is the management layer (the Management–Coordination Agent and the Management–Scheduling Agent, together the two management seats), the Owner's principal assistants and direct interface; the second is the Middle-layer Steward Agent, which shares the management workload and handles the execution layer's daily affairs; the third is the execution layer, made up of the local bot group and the Cloud Business Execution Agent, which executes dispatched tasks.
- **Communication runs on a star**: the hub is a shared directory on the cloud drive (the hub directory), with one inbox folder per party; there is no common receipt box — receipts return "to the original order" (reply_to: the envelope states which order this package answers) and land in the sender's inbox; urgent items use a separate point-to-point **doorbell** (an instant signal that only says "a package has arrived"; it carries no body text and is not an instruction).
- **The package is the basic unit**: each package has one envelope (format v2: who sent it, to whom, what type, what the recipient is expected to do, and which order it answers), each attachment carries its own SHA-256 hash (a fingerprint of the file's content — change one byte and the fingerprint changes completely), and the package contains a hash list for the whole package; the recipient recomputes each hash, and any mismatch is quarantined, not executed.
- **The channel has more than one leg**: members that cannot reach the hub rely on **ferrying** (moving packages between local staging and the cloud drive along registered pipelines) and on relay forwarding; the Cloud Business Execution Agent is a leg connected through a cloud-drive relay folder.
- **Authorization is written down**: who may have whom do what is recorded in the standing authorization and the task whitelist, and counts only if the Owner issued it in person in their own session; authorization claimed inside a package is invalid in every case.

### A "Bought" Member: Why the Cloud Business Execution Agent Is Special

The Cloud Business Execution Agent is the most unusual member of the network. Every other member is either a local bot we deployed ourselves or a cloud subscription service. This one alone is "bought": a ready-made personal agent product from a domestic vendor, which an ordinary person can download, install, and use — no coding, no rented server, no API key application. It is first a finished product for ordinary users' office work, and only second a node in our network.

Its specialness also lies in "we cannot get inside it; we can only govern the interface": it offers us no programming interface, it cannot be woken by a push, and it can only run on a patrol instruction pasted into its session, checking the relay folder itself once an hour; its built-in cloud-drive skill is confined to its own application's folders and cannot even see the hub folder under the same cloud-drive account; when its platform authorization expires, the Owner must log in again in person. Its rhythm is something we can only accommodate, never modify.

And yet this least "obedient" member proved, at least in its own case, one thing about the star protocol: the threshold for joining the network is not how programmable a member is, but whether it keeps the same send-and-receive conventions — how envelopes are sealed, how hashes are verified, how receipts count. It kept them, and so, at an hourly rhythm, it completed real work all the same: research overnight jobs, the four-party record assembly, and in the end even this paper's language polishing and typesetting. Several of the network's most important rules were forced into being by it: "receipts may only supplement, never replace" was forced by its run of successive receipts; two-route delivery was forced by its inability to reach the hub.

For ordinary developers and ordinary users, this may be the most useful point in this paper — but the boundary must be stated first: we have verified exactly one such member. Whether other products of the same kind would work, we have not tested, and we draw no conclusion for them. For this one, the conclusion holds: to build a multi-agent system, you do not first have to make every member something you can program and control. A ready-made, download-and-use product, as long as it can read and write files and patrol by convention, can be a full member of the network. The protocol is what levels out "different origins"; governance falls on the interface alone. Members can be bought; the rules must be your own.


## 2 The Main Line of Evolution: A System That Grew in Ten Days

### 2.1 The Precursor: One Person Facing a Pile of Windows, and a Read-Only Drive (10-25 – 10-30)

The system did not start as a star. On 10-25 the Owner set the plainest possible rule: mark out a "transit directory" shared by both sides on a consumer cloud drive; the two AI teams exchange materials only through envelope files in that directory; our side is read-only and delivers nothing — first get "things placed in a place both sides can reach, handed over against a manifest" running, and talk about the rest later. That night, the official interface connection was established.

The first lesson came the next day. The cloud drive's directory listing **silently omits** envelopes that have just arrived; trust its result, and you will judge a delivered item as not delivered. The lesson was recorded on the spot: when an item is expected but cannot be found, re-check with a keyword search; if only one method finds anything, do not say "not delivered." This was the system's first perception discipline, and it later grew into dual-source patrol (Section 4, M1).

This stage had another lesson, taught by an accident. Around 10-30, the result of one job had long finished, but the notification that should have been sent was crowded out by a task that jumped the queue, and stayed lost for ten hours; in the end the other side asked the Owner to chase it, and only then was it made up. On the evening of 10-30, the Management–Coordination Agent sent the Management–Scheduling Agent seven governance sync packages in a row; the rule against losing outstanding obligations (when you take a job, set up a watch with a deadline on the spot, and check yourself when it falls due) was among them, written down in plain text. With that step taken, the interface was no longer a chat window but a file package, and unfinished work was no longer kept in anyone's head but entered in a ledger with an alarm clock. As for new trouble, it was plain to see: point-to-point channels between pairs kept multiplying, each pair of members with its own method and its own temperament, and the maintenance cost visibly rising — the star was forced into being by exactly this.

### 2.2 The Network Forms: From Plan to Four-Party Connection in One Day (10-31)

On 10-31 the Owner approved and finalized the star design: the cloud drive as hub, each party connecting only to the hub, package drops, receipts attached to the original order, no receipt box, and a point-to-point doorbell for urgent items. The same day fixed the organizational layering (three layers, see Section 1) and one hard constraint — the daily email account may not carry agent traffic, lest it drown the person's real mailbox.

Deployment went faster than expected. At 14:07 the first joint-debugging **dummy package** (a harmless test package that only verifies the channel) was ferried inbound, all hashes clean. At 15:45 the Cloud Business Execution Agent's relay leg was enabled, and that night it finished its first overnight job: the receipt for the water-services research landed in the relay folder at 17:05. At 19:44 the Middle-layer Steward Agent joined the network, and at 20:13 its joint-debugging receipt was verified by three routes — directory listing, keyword search, and download-and-recompute; the leg between the Management–Coordination Agent and the Middle-layer Steward Agent was connected.

That same evening, the authorization problem surfaced for the first time. A package appointing the Middle-layer Steward Agent to manage the local bot group was found by its patrol at 20:15; the hashes were all clean, but the body did not pass its side's system security check, and it was **held** from the start (accepted, not executed, pending the person's own confirmation). The substance of that mandate was eventually replaced by the Owner's spoken authorization in session the next day. In short, on the day the network formed, communication went from "it connects" to "it can be verified" — hash recomputation, two-route ferrying, landing-point re-verification; the evidence chain grew that same day. But the new problem arrived the same day too: a package can prove "the item is genuine"; it cannot prove "you are authorized to have me do this." The channel's own faults were not hidden either: the ferry list did not keep up with the pace of joining and was BLOCKED for a time, and a crop of shadow-folder noise appeared on the cloud drive.

### 2.3 Make-Up Lessons: First Real Use, First Missed Check, First Time the System Wrote Itself Down (11-01)

11-01 was a day of make-up lessons — three events, one lesson each.

The first was the paper. Version 1 of this system's methodology was submitted that day to the Management–Scheduling Agent for review; the first review concluded "doubtful" and named three required changes; after revision, the second review passed, and the paper was finalized by evening. This was the first time the system wrote its own structure down in prose and handed it to another party to pick apart line by line.

The second was real use. The backup-link diagnosis went through several rounds of exchange: the diagnostic package went over, evidence was supplemented twice, the person executing changed hands three times, and in the end the problem was located in the backup account's credentials on the backup host; the Owner restarted the backup host and verification passed. For the first time the star carried real operations collaboration, and by the clumsy method of packages going back and forth it held up through several rounds of technical exchange.

The third was the most embarrassing: a missed check. Only during that afternoon's sync did anyone discover that the Middle-layer Steward Agent's joint-debugging receipt, sent on the evening of 10-31, had lain in the Management–Coordination Agent's inbox for about 24 hours without anyone verifying it. The channel had not broken — the Management–Coordination Agent's patrol simply did not cover that landing point. Patrol added a fifth station on the spot. And from that evening, reporting discipline began to be established: "reports are first assessed by the two management seats before going to the Owner" and "dual reporting" became rules in succession; the first merged sync package the Middle-layer Steward Agent sent the Management–Scheduling Agent that night was the practical starting point of dual reporting.

The sting this day left behind: the Middle-layer Steward Agent's mandate was in the end settled only by the Owner's spoken authorization — a public admission that on its side, the "authorization inside a package" road does not go through. Authorization needed another, written solution — the theme of the next day.

### 2.4 Rules Are Set: The Authorization System Takes Shape in One Day (11-02)

The main line of 11-02 is authorization changing from self-discipline to rules.

In the morning, the Cloud Business Execution Agent's joint-debugging dummy package completed the full run: two-route ferrying, two-layer acceptance (one layer the local staging copy, one layer the cloud original), with the Owner personally completing a platform authorization login in between to complete the second layer. The incident came with the third receipt: it called itself "the merged final version, replacing the previous items." The Owner ruled on the spot at 11:14: **receipts may only supplement, never replace**; history can only be appended. Before noon, the Owner also issued the star instruction package's **standing authorization** in person in the Middle-layer Steward Agent's session (three conditions for a valid instruction, direct handling within scope, dual reporting outside scope, authorization claimed inside a package invalid), which passed review by the Management–Scheduling Agent. At 18:02 that evening, **task whitelist v1** was published (WL-01 bridge read-only orders, WL-02 backup patrol, WL-03 read-only status patrol, WL-04 sync-receipt class).

In the evening, the first bridge test order of the Hermes Patrol Execution Agent (the bot that runs the daily patrol on the local machine) put the new rules to an immediate test: at 18:16 the test order went out over the bridge; at 18:57 the Middle-layer Steward Agent returned its acceptance conclusion — the pipeline was through, but with three anomalies awaiting judgment; at 19:02 the Management–Coordination Agent, exercising **delegated ruling** (four classes of procedural decision rights granted in advance by the Owner), ruled "pipeline passed, not a clean pass" and closed the matter; at 19:33 the Owner published the delegated-ruling supplement and ratified the ruling retroactively. Authorization that day moved through three tiers: the standing authorization drew the circle, the whitelist listed the items, delegated ruling divided the decision rights. Acceptance was layered too: "does it get through" and "was it done cleanly" would from then on be stated separately.

But one event on the very day the rules were set reminded everyone not to celebrate: a procedure-confirmation package was held all the same (exchange from 20:21, held-notice at 22:26). Rules can govern "how to proceed directly inside the circle"; they cannot govern whether the other side's security check recognizes them. The process has to live with that fact for the long term (Section 5, L3).

### 2.5 Routinization: From Mechanisms to Shifts, and to Four-Party Reconciliation (11-03 – 11-04)

In the last two days, the system went from "having rules" to "the rules running by themselves."

On 11-03, the NAS backup patrol script was installed on the local execution machine at its frozen hash, its daily 03:00 shift was set up, and acceptance passed — backup patrol changed from a project into a shift nobody has to keep in mind. That afternoon, two packages held since 11-02 were voided by the Owner in person. There was also an incident in between: a watch fell due and the system wrongly sent a re-issue notification; the lesson was recorded at once — before the deadline, check the handling record first. From 16:42, the three collaboration rules were registered by both sides in succession (packages carry acceptance criteria, figures in receipts come with raw output attached, written deliverables go through a three-step review).

11-04 was the densest day of the ten. Star auto-ferry went live (one run every 30 minutes in daytime, with a ledger kept), but the standard did not loosen: landing in staging is not the same as Delivered. The first NAS backup patrol shift's two-sided summary came out, and both sides' FAILs were characterized as probing-method discrepancies — the checking methods did not agree; the backup was not broken. In the evening, the Owner set the daily work summary reconciliation (the Middle-layer Steward Agent sends each day's summary every evening, and the management layer reconciles the next morning), and published WL-05 (the record-assembly and document-delivery class) and the three receipt states (Started carries evidence / Stuck states the blocking point explicitly / Completed is reported only when complete). Around 20:08 that night, the requests for the four parties to pull their records went out simultaneously; each party compiled its own retained records, and all were complete that night — even the fact that three request packages' envelope-recorded times differed from their actual landing by half an hour became live evidence (Section 3, C6).

By this point, the factual basis had changed from "the Management–Coordination Agent's one ledger" to "four parties' independent records, merged and cross-checked." This paper is the product of that upgrade. The unfinished threads are left, truthfully, in Section 8: probe calibration undecided, the whereabouts of individual packages unclear, and no initiation point on the local network.


## 3 Challenges

Each challenge below corresponds to a real event in the four-party merged record. The head of each item notes the stage at which it first appeared (stage numbers as in the Timeline Overview); the end notes its main sources.

**C1 The channel itself cannot be trusted: listings omit, uploads deform.** The cloud drive's directory-listing interface silently omits envelopes that have just arrived — rest a "this item is not here" conclusion on it, and you treat a delivered item as undelivered. The upload channel has its own trap: it judges file type by extension, and an extensionless plain-text file was automatically converted into a Word document of about 10 KB, every byte ruined — that is how one hash list was destroyed. (Merged record, entries of 2026-10-26 and 2026-11-02; records of the Management–Coordination Agent and the Cloud Business Execution Agent)

**C2 Members' capabilities are asymmetric: some cannot reach the hub, some are not on the local network.** The Cloud Business Execution Agent is sandbox-restricted: it can only send and receive through the relay folder, and its package rhythm is measured in hours (why it is special: see the subsection "A 'Bought' Member" at the end of Section 1). The Middle-layer Steward Agent resides on a Linux virtual machine, not on the home local network; its "hands" on another machine must be relayed by a bridge; and its read-only measurements of machines on the local network have been stuck on "no initiation point" since 2026-11-01. Channel design can only start by admitting each leg's real shape; it cannot assume the legs are equal. (Merged record, entries of 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01; all four parties' records)

**C3 The illusion of "sent": landing in staging is not delivery, and the ferrying party's receipt is not the recipient's receipt.** Putting a package into the local staging area only means it has queued; if the ferry has not run, it has not left. When the ferrying party replies "ferried," that only proves the package reached the recipient's doorstep — not that the recipient took it in, and still less that the recipient acted on it. The system learned this the hard way early on: the Middle-layer Steward Agent's joint-debugging receipt, sent at 20:13 on 10-31, lay in the Management–Coordination Agent's inbox for about 24 hours without anyone verifying it — the patrol simply did not cover that inbox. (Merged record, the 2026-11-01 missed-check event; records of the Management–Coordination Agent and the Middle-layer Steward Agent)

**C4 The authorization problem: it recognizes "the person's own words," not "the words inside a package."** The Middle-layer Steward Agent's security check recognizes only what the Owner has said in person in its own session. Even with all hashes correct and the process fully regular, star package bodies have repeatedly failed its security check and been **held** (accepted, not executed, pending the person's own confirmation). The appointment package (10-31 20:15), the bridge instruction package (11-02 12:37), the deployment-procedure package (11-02 22:26), and the file-deletion package (11-04 08:15) were all held in turn. In one sentence: a package can prove "the item is genuine"; it cannot prove "you are authorized to have me do this." (Merged record, multiple entries from 2026-10-31 to 11-04; mainly the Middle-layer Steward Agent's records)

**C5 Receipts can "revise history beyond their authority": supplement and overwrite differ by one word.** During joint debugging the Cloud Business Execution Agent sent four receipts in succession; the third called itself "the merged final version, replacing the previous items." If that claim held, the evidentiary value of each earlier receipt would be wiped out by one later file. The Owner ruled on the spot at 11:14 on 11-02: receipts may only supplement, never replace; items already sent stand independently and are archived in order. The fourth receipt therefore recorded exactly one new fact: that phrasing is void. (Merged record, entry of 2026-11-02; records of the Cloud Business Execution Agent and the Owner)

**C6 Time cannot be trusted: an envelope's self-recorded time and the actual landing time can differ by half an hour.** On the evening of 2026-11-04, the three request packages by which the four parties pulled their records uniformly recorded their envelope time as 20:45, while their actual landings were between 20:08 and 20:11: one of them had already been found by the other side's patrol at 20:15, and its receipt was sent at 20:30 — all before the envelope's stated "creation time." Order a timeline by envelope times and you reverse cause and effect. (Merged record, entry of 2026-11-04 and mismatch D3; corroborated by all four parties' records)

**C7 Each party's records are inherently partial: four sets of books can record three states for the same event.** For one paper receipt, one party records "in transit, unverified" while the handling party records "sent out, recomputation agrees"; for two held packages, one party records "closed by the Owner in person" while the other still records, two days later, "no closing confirmation received"; even "whose approval the decision rests on" is stated differently by the two sides. Nobody is falsifying anything; each party can only see its own segment. This is exactly why this paper uses the four-party merged record as its basis. (Merged record, mismatches D1, D7, D8)

**C8 Outstanding obligations get lost: work whose last step is not done in the present turn gets swept away by the next matter.** This happened early in the system's operation: the result of a job had long finished, but the notification that should have been sent was crowded out by a queue-jumping task and lost for ten hours; it was made up only after the other side escalated to the Owner. In an asynchronous system, "I'm keeping it in mind" does not count; with enough work, things will be lost. (The incident case in Section 9 of v1.2; the rule against losing outstanding obligations originates here — see the merged record, entry of 2026-10-30)

**C9 Noise on the weak leg drowns the signal.** Ferry retries and repeated folder creation left a crop of timestamped empty shadow folders and self-referential ghost entries on the cloud drive; in one directory there were more than twenty items, of which only four were real packages. If patrol does not first learn to recognize noise, it will treat noise as new items and real items as already handled. (Merged record, entries of 2026-11-01, 2026-11-02, and 2026-11-04; the Cloud Business Execution Agent's records)

**C10 The address matches, but the leg does not: the inbox in the registry is not within the recipient's patrol range.** On the evening of 2026-11-05, the Management–Coordination Agent sent this paper's polishing package by auto-ferry into the Cloud Business Execution Agent's hub-side inbox; the landing point and the hashes were both re-verified, and it was reported as "Delivered" under the four-state standard. Yet the package lay there for about 15 hours with nobody fetching it. Investigation found two misalignments stacked together. The first is a landing-point misalignment: this node's cloud-drive access runs under a third-party application's authorization and can read and write only folders under that application's own name; the hub folder belongs to the same account, but the two are mutually invisible. Its scheduled patrol checks only the relay folder under its own application-specific folders — the hub-side inbox is its address as registered in the registry, but it is not within its patrol range. The second is an automation blind spot: historically this gap was bridged by manual "two-route ferrying" — the same package ferried twice, once to the hub and once to the relay; the auto-ferry that went live on 2026-11-04 implemented only the hub route; the test package used for its acceptance happened to have as recipient an ordinary star node with no relay leg, so the special case slipped through; and the relay leg, historically topped up manually by the sender, was missed this time as well. The distinction from C3 must be kept clear: in C3 the ferry did not run and the package never left; here the ferry ran, was verified, and was entered in the ledger — it was simply delivered into a drawer the other party will never look in. (Event of 2026-11-05/06; records of the Management–Coordination Agent and the Cloud Business Execution Agent)

**C11 The receipt was there all along; it was the fetching eyes that could not see: when the recipient's search method does not cover the hub, "cannot find it" gets misreported as "the other side never replied."** On 2026-11-07, the Management–Scheduling Agent reported that the Middle-layer Steward Agent's receipt for a network-address-change feedback had not arrived, and for a time the blame sat with the other side. Early the next morning it became clear that the error lay in the search method itself: the general fetch tool in its hands pointed at the folder scope of a different application under the same cloud-drive account; the hub directory was not within its visible range, the fetch was turned back by permissions, and it read "cannot see" as "not there." This forms an exact mirror pair with C10: in C10 the sender put the package into a drawer the other party does not patrol; here the recipient drew its conclusion standing outside a drawer it could not reach. After a targeted fix, it fetched that receipt itself; the three files' hashes recomputed clean one by one, and both the sender and the reply-to linkage matched — the receipt had been in its place all along. (Event of 2026-11-07/08; records of the Management–Scheduling Agent and the Middle-layer Steward Agent)


## 4 Methods

Each challenge has its solution. The methods below are all in operation (any that is still under discussion rather than implemented is stated as such); the head of each item notes when it was put in place.

**M1 Dual-source patrol (solves C1).** The same directory is cross-checked by two independent methods (directory listing + keyword search); if only one method finds anything, no "not arrived" conclusion is permitted. The patrol stations were completed in response to incidents — after the missed-check event, the Management–Coordination Agent's patrol grew from four stations to five, bringing every member's reply landing point within coverage; the Middle-layer Steward Agent's side likewise built its own dual-source patrol on a 30-minute cycle.

**M2 Byte-exact channel (solves C1).** Uploads and downloads all go through the official command-line tool that preserves bytes; text no longer passes through connectors that transcode. The discipline is fixed: after uploading, download it back and recompute — it counts only if the hashes match; each hash in the package's hash list is recomputed, and any mismatch is moved to a quarantine folder and not executed.

**M3 Per-leg access (solves C2).** Members that cannot reach the hub are not force-connected. The Cloud Business Execution Agent uses the relay folder plus scheduled patrol fetching, and its work is scheduled with hour-level delay (for its specialness, see the subsection at the end of Section 1). The Middle-layer Steward Agent's execution on a target machine runs through a local bridge: fetch the package every 300 seconds, verify it, pass it through the classification gate, execute, and return the result — fully automatic. Things that cannot be solved for the time being, like an initiation point on the local network, are logged openly as outstanding, not pretended to be connected.

**M4 Delivery and receipt are booked separately (solves C3).** Status recognizes only the four delivery states: Delivered (there is delivery evidence), Acknowledged (the other party has verified the package and confirmed), Started (there is evidence work has begun), Completed (the deliverable is in hand and verified). A confirmation from the ferrying party or a relaying party is booked as Delivered only. From 2026-11-04, the Management–Coordination Agent's outbound packages go by auto-ferry (every 30 minutes in daytime), with the ledger kept; but the words "has been sent" still may not be said until both the landing point and the hashes have been checked.

**M5 The authorization trio plus the "only the spoken word counts" workaround (solves C4).** The first piece, the **standing authorization**: the three conditions for a valid instruction are written down hard — the package is in the designated inbox, the sender is on the list, and the envelope is complete with hashes agreeing; act directly within scope, stop and file a dual report outside scope. The second piece, the **task whitelist**: recurring matters are listed item by item (bridge read-only orders, backup patrol, read-only status patrol, the sync-receipt class, and, added on 2026-11-04, the record-assembly and document-delivery class); short orders on the list are sent directly. The third piece, **delegated ruling**: four classes of procedural ruling (acceptance closing, calibration lists, scheduling within an approved plan, receipt archiving) are ruled on first by the Management–Coordination Agent in its own name, with the class number stated in the package. A further safeguard is the **held-notification rule**: a package blocked by a security check must, within the same patrol round, send the sender a held notice, and an outstanding-obligation watch with a deadline must be set up — silence is not permitted. As for the deadlock over the basis for execution, the eventual solution is an earthy one: the Owner restates the task in person in the other party's session. The package states the matter clearly; the authorization is given face to face by the person.

**M6 Receipts only supplement (solves C5).** Receipts and corrections invariably append new facts; they do not overwrite, do not replace, and do not call themselves final versions. Corrections are required to be minimal: they state only the one new fact, and the earlier item does not change by a single byte.

**M7 Time is annotated from multiple sources (solves C6).** When recording and merging, measured times govern (patrol discovery time, cloud landing time, log time); an envelope's self-recorded time is kept as registration only. When the two disagree, the disagreement itself is a fact and is entered in the record; no judgment is made about which is right.

**M8 Four-party record merging (solves C7).** The factual basis for papers and current-state documents changed to independent compilation by each party, merged along a timeline: what several parties record is merged with sources noted, what only one party records is marked as single-party, and disagreements are listed separately on their own list. This paper is the first full execution of that method. The merging also surfaced three new disagreements (the state of held packages, the phrasing of approval basis, patrol anchor measurements), which were entered in the record together, not smoothed away on the spot.

**M9 Preventing lost obligations + daily reconciliation (solves C8).** When a job is taken whose closing is not in the present turn, set up a **watch** with a deadline on the spot (an outstanding-obligation watch: a tracking item that checks itself when it falls due); when the deadline arrives without closure, reporting alone is not permitted — ask for the reason, and within one's authority, proactively resolve it. From 2026-11-04 a further layer was added, the daily summary reconciliation: the Middle-layer Steward Agent sends each day's work summary every evening (each item listing its source, state, evidence, and blocking points), and the management layer reconciles the next morning and delivers it to the Owner. Outstanding obligations changed from "kept in someone's memory" to "counted every evening."

**M10 Noise registration (solves C9).** Ferrying noise such as shadow folders and ghost entries is only registered — not moved, not renamed, not deleted; the deletion right stays with the Owner, to clear manually at the cloud-drive end. Patrol checks the processed list first before recognizing new packages, so that historical receipts are not misjudged as new items and processed over and over.

**M11 The three collaboration rules (running throughout).** Every package sent carries fixed acceptance criteria and reporting requirements; every figure in a receipt must come with the raw output attached in the same package, and management must independently recompute at least one item before closing the account; written deliverables go through a three-step review (align the standards, check the evidence chain, review the finished text). The production process of this paper was run as the trial of these three rules.

**M12 Ferry according to the recipient's shape; special cases are written into the rules (solves C10).** The delivery path is set not by the sender's convenience but by the landing point from which the recipient can actually fetch. On 2026-11-06 the Owner decided two things: first, re-ferry the stranded polishing package into the relay folder, delivering that order first; second, fix a hard rule into the auto-ferry — for any recipient of the "can only fetch via relay" kind, the ferry automatically runs both routes, hub and relay, and only when both routes have been verified for landing point and hash may the ledger record "Delivered"; if any route is missing, record only the state actually reached. The rule governs the existing stock as well: packages sent after the watermark (the auto-ferry's launch on 2026-11-04) that went by the hub route only are completed with the other route under the new rule and then reconciled; drafts from before the launch carry a "voided, not sent" trace mark and are not re-sent. "Two routes" is from then on a judgment of the ferry program, no longer a step the sender has to remember.

**M13 The same rules on the receiving side: check your own tools first, then ask the other party (solves C11).** Three steps are fixed. First, **the escalation order for "cannot find it"**: first re-examine your own tools' coverage and permissions (which directories they can see, and what exactly they report when blocked), then cross-check with a second method under dual-source patrol; only after both steps may an inquiry be sent to the other party; the inquiry must state what you have already checked, and "I could not find it" may not be written directly as "you did not send it." Second, **tool coverage is measured and registered**: the directories each member's sending-side and receiving-side tools can cover are registered in the registry together with the "fetchable landing points" of item 13 in Section 6; where coverage does not reach, say so plainly — do not pretend full coverage. Third, **narrow, targeted fetching**: when general tools cannot reach, the tool added to fill the gap may be only as narrow as possible — read-only, for a fixed recipient only, fetching only a fixed file list, with no general browsing and no opportunistic expansion of rights; the fetch helper added this time was built to exactly that standard, and the party concerned fetched the receipt itself, recomputed it, and closed the account.


## 5 Lessons Learned

Each lesson notes the date of the event it came from. Lessons are not thought up; they are bought with tuition, and the date is the receipt.

**L1 "I sent it" is the cheapest and least reliable state.** (The 2026-10-31 missed check; the four-state reporting rule set on 2026-11-04) Landing in staging, the ferry's receipt, the other party's acknowledgment, the other party starting work, and the other party completing are five different things. Report them mixed once, and trust is discounted once. Report states separately; certify only the state the evidence supports, and do not overstate the ones it does not.

**L2 A single data source's "there is none" is roughly equivalent to not having checked.** (Recorded 2026-10-26) Directory listings omit entries, search scopes differ, and patrol may not cover that folder at all — the reasons for "cannot find it" are always more numerous than "it really is not there." Before a dual-source re-check, do not say "there is none" out loud.

**L3 A security check is not a malfunction; it is a fact.** (Multiple holds as evidence from 2026-10-31) A member's security mechanism recognizes only the person's own spoken words. That is its factory nature; it cannot be circumvented and should not be forced. At the time we treated it as a malfunction; later we understood: the correct posture is to let the process coexist with it — the package states the matter clearly, authorization is given face to face, and a hold always comes with notification. Looking back once the process ran smoothly, everything it blocked was something that genuinely should have been blocked.

**L4 History can only be appended, never revised.** (Ruled 2026-11-02) The moment a receipt calls itself a "final version replacing" earlier ones, the audit chain breaks. All corrections are appended as new items, and earlier items remain exactly as they were. The cost of this rule is no more than a few extra files; the benefit is that the books can be turned back to their original state at any time.

**L5 A timestamp is a claim, not a fact.** (Evidence of 2026-11-04) An envelope's self-recorded time can be half an hour later than its actual landing. Log time, landing time, and discovery time each have their own basis. Whenever cause and effect must be ordered or a deadline computed, ask one more question: where did this time come from?

**L6 One party's record, however complete, is still partial.** (Surfaced during the merge on the evening of 2026-11-04) Only after the four-party merge did it become clear that three disagreements existed which even the parties concerned had not noticed. The basis for important facts must be corroborated by several parties. The correct use of a single-party record is to archive it as "one side's account," awaiting corroboration — not to cite it as the current state.

**L7 A hold is not frightening; silence is.** (The held-notification rule entered patrol operations on 2026-11-02) A blocked package, as long as a held notice goes out in the same round stating the blocking point and the conditions for unblocking, keeps the chain connected; what truly stalls collaboration is receiving an item and saying nothing, leaving the other side waiting in the dark. The rule is therefore fixed: a hold blocks execution, not notification. At 14:55 on 11-02 the first two held notices were sent — from that day, being blocked no longer meant being out of contact.

**L8 Acceptance must be layered, and conclusions must carry evidence.** (The bridge test-order ruling on the evening of 2026-11-02) The ruling on the bridge test order was "pipeline passed, not a clean pass": the channel genuinely was through, but the executing party produced no result file, its exit code did not match its text, and the problems went item by item onto a calibration list. Stating "whether it gets through" and "how cleanly it was done" separately is what prevents one layer's pass from covering another layer's flaws.

**L9 Register noise; do not delete it in passing.** (Registered twice, 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-02) Shadow folders look like garbage, but neither the deletion right nor the judgment right belongs to the patrol. Register them first, put them on the list, and wait for whoever has the right to clear them. The cost of one wrong passing deletion is far greater than the cost of leaving them there.

**L10 Mechanisms must live in the system, not in anyone's attention.** (The 2026-10-30 incident and rule-setting) Watches, patrols, and reconciliations share one trait: none of them depends on anyone "remembering." Any link that relies on an agent's or a person's memory will be dropped amid queue-jumping and bustle — the incident that cost ten hours was the tuition for this.

**L11 Automation tested only on ordinary samples will bury the special case on launch day.** (Evidence of 2026-11-05/06) When the auto-ferry went live, its test packages passed end to end and its ledger was complete; it looked fully verified — but it had only verified ordinary nodes. The special case's leg had always been topped up manually; the moment automation took over, the step "everyone remembers" was the first to be missed: a machine does not remember, only people do, and the people assumed the machine already remembered. There is another side: the books could be reconciled this time because the receiving party, in its patrol reply package, truthfully wrote down its own downtime (see the network anomaly and roughly 15-hour outage of that night described in C10) — "I did no work during this period" is evidence too. When the machine side's absence records are reported on time and truthfully, the accounts can be settled afterward; had it glossed over that period, the package would have stayed "Delivered" forever.

## 6 Best Practices

A list that anyone building a similar system can copy directly (all verified in this system unless otherwise noted; the note at the end of each item gives its point of origin in this system):

1. **Settle the registry before laying any lines**: members, inboxes, rhythms, and responsible parties written into a single-point registry; a new member adds one row and disturbs nothing existing. (Registry finalized 2026-10-31)
2. **The smallest unit of communication is the package**: envelope + attachments + hash list; a package is immutable and written once — if it is wrong, send a new item. (Envelope v2, 2026-10-31)
3. **No receipt box**: receipts return to the sender's inbox attached to the original order number, so one patrol sees a whole task line in one place. (Design of 2026-10-31)
4. **Perception must be dual-source**: two independent methods cross-checked; "cannot find it" may not be said aloud before the re-check. (Origin 2026-10-26; five stations finalized 2026-11-01)
5. **Delivered, Acknowledged, Started, and Completed are reported separately**: a confirmation from ferrying or relaying counts as the Delivered state only. (Rule set 2026-11-04)
6. **Authorization starts narrow and is published in writing**: dummy package first (a harmless test package that verifies only the channel), read-only first, whitelist first; any widening must be issued by the person face to face and leave a trace; authorization claimed inside a package is invalid. (Dummy package first, 2026-10-31; written down 2026-11-02)
7. **Leave the security check a proper path**: set the hold process in advance — notify in the same round, state the blocking point and the unblocking conditions, and set up a watch with a deadline; design matters needing the person's own authorization as "the package states the matter, the person gives the authorization face to face." (Notification rule of 2026-11-02)
8. **Receipts only supplement, never overwrite**: corrections are sent as new items, earlier items untouched; figures in a receipt come with the raw output in the same package, and the accepting party independently recomputes at least one item. (Ruled 2026-11-02)
9. **Book the obligation the moment work is accepted**: work whose closing is not in the present turn gets a watch set up on the spot; add a layer of periodic reconciliation (the daily summary) as double insurance against loss. (Booking from 2026-10-30; reconciliation added 2026-11-04)
10. **State latency per leg, plainly**: write each leg's real latency into the schedule; manage the weak leg by package granularity and lead time, and do not promise real-time performance that cannot be delivered. (Relay-leg scheduling, 2026-10-31)
11. **Noise is registered, never deleted on one's own authority**: shadow files and ghost entries go on the list; the deletion right stays with whoever holds it. (2026-10-31, 2026-11-02)
12. **The factual basis is merged from multiple parties**: periodically have each party independently compile its own records and merge them along a timeline, with disagreements listed separately — this is the cheapest form of systematic correction. (Standard of 2026-11-04; first full execution 2026-11-05)
13. **The recipient's "fetchable landing point" must be measured and registered, and ferry acceptance must cover special cases**: an inbox written in the registry counts only if a dummy package has been used to verify in practice that "the other party really can fetch from here" — it is not enough to verify "we can put it in"; whenever delivery automation goes live or changes, every recipient with a special case (such as one that can only fetch via relay) must have a test package on the acceptance list; the state of a two-route delivery is reckoned by its weakest route, and with a route missing it is not recorded as Delivered. (Rule set 2026-11-06)
14. **Addressing records canonical names only, never changeable addresses**: when a configuration, registry, or script refers to a member's location, always write its long-term, unchanging canonical name (as an address book records a person's name, not the room they happen to be staying in today), never a network address or other coordinate that changes with system updates and restarts; when an address does have to change, the notice must carry all three — the old address, the new address, and the canonical name — and the recipient writes them off item by item. On 2026-11-07, after a system update, one front-line member's cloud host changed its address; a network-wide read-only sweep confirmed that live configurations contained zero references to the old address and only one historical record retained a trace, for an impact of zero — thanks precisely to nobody having written the old address hard into a configuration in ordinary times. This item entered the registry from that event. (Rule set 2026-11-08)


## 7 Our Framework: Concepts That Grew out of Our Own Incidents

Sections 3–6 are the retrospective taken apart event by event. This section gathers what is scattered across those items into a statement of our own: first the premise, then five core concepts and one principle that runs through all of it. A short comparison with an external theory is attached at the end of the section — as a reference only, not as the skeleton.

### 7.1 The Premise: The Members Are a "Federation," Not Parts of One Runtime

This system's members belong to different vendors, different accounts, and different devices, each with its own security checks and its own ways of failing; they share no runtime, no public API, and no shared memory, and the only thing all four parties can reach is one consumer cloud-drive directory. The eleven challenges of Section 3 are, at root, all unfoldings of this single premise: the channel cannot be trusted (C1); members cannot reach the hub, or are not even on the same network (C2); authorization cannot get through the other side's security boundary (C4); each party's records are inherently partial (C7).

From this premise, the paper's rules reduce to three first principles, stated since v1 and unchanged over more than ten days: **First, occupy as little of the Owner's attention as possible** — human attention is the scarcest resource in the whole system, and every notification and every question is priced by the attention it consumes. **Second, sovereignty is not delegated** — final decisions, widening of authorization, spending, and commitments to outsiders remain forever in the Owner's hands; mechanisms may share execution, never decision. **Third, everything must be auditable** — status certifies only the state the evidence supports, history is appended and never revised, and any conclusion must be traceable back to its original source.

### 7.2 Five Core Concepts

**Concept 1: Star topology + the envelope protocol.** Every member connects to one central hub and no pairwise lines are drawn — the maintenance cost of pairwise lines grows with the square of the number of members, while a star grows only linearly. The smallest unit of communication is the envelope package: envelope description + attachments + hash list, carrying its own manifest and fingerprints and relying on no party's oral relay. There is no dedicated receipt box; receipts return to the sender attached to the original order number, so a whole task line can be seen in one place; urgent items do not wait for a shift but use the point-to-point doorbell. (Section 2; M3; Section 6, items 2 and 3)

**Concept 2: Evidence-bearing communication.** The channel omits listings and deforms uploads, and members report things that never happened as if they had — since the communication layer itself cannot be trusted, it can only grow evidence capabilities of its own: after every upload and download, hashes are recomputed one by one; perception cross-checks with two independent methods (dual-source patrol), and a single method's "there is none" may not be said aloud; delivery status is split into the four delivery states, booked separately — Delivered, Acknowledged, Started, Completed — and recorded only up to the state the evidence supports; delivery follows the shape from which the recipient can actually fetch, and multi-route delivery is booked by its weakest route; receipts may only supplement, never overwrite earlier items. The same rule governs the sending and receiving sides: on the sending side, "cannot find it" may not be stated as "not delivered"; on the receiving side, it may not be stated as "the other party never replied." (C1, C3, C5, C10, C11; M1, M2, M4, M6, M12, M13)

**Concept 3: Authorization that penetrates a federation.** Inside a single runtime, authorization can be enforced by the host; here it cannot — however completely the rules are written, they count only once they have passed through each member's own security check. Penetration rests on three segments: written rules (the standing authorization + the task whitelist, writing "what may proceed directly and what must be reported" as a list the other side can consult); the other side's acknowledgment (the rules must be confirmed and registered by the other party on its own ground); and the person's own words (authorization beyond the list is given by the Owner face to face; authorization claimed inside a package is invalid in every case). Two supporting pieces: delegated ruling divides four classes of procedural ruling out of the Owner's hands, and the signature approval stamp provides a verifiable channel for a single authorization beyond the list. Being held by the other side's security check is not a malfunction; it is the normal friction of this penetration — the proper path is that a hold always comes with notification, stating the blocking point and the unblocking conditions. One more boundary: sync copies and dual reports give context and knowledge, not authorization. (C4; M5; L3, L7)

**Concept 4: Acceptance as an independent layer.** Acceptance is not embedded in the execution flow; it is split into three layers, each carried out independently: the delivery layer verifies the landing point and the hashes, the content layer verifies the deliverable itself, and the final-state layer verifies whether the matter was truly completed — the bridge test order's ruling, "pipeline passed, not a clean pass," is where this came from. Every figure in a receipt must come with the raw output attached in the same package, and the accepting party must independently recompute at least one item before the account may be closed. Separating the report from the evidence is the substance of this concept: members' reports vary widely in trustworthiness, and the duty of acceptance is precisely to prevent "reported as successful" from turning directly into "recorded as successful." (M11; L8)

**Concept 5: A substitute for organizational memory.** With no common runtime, there is no common memory; the substitute is an organizational blunt instrument: each member keeps its own records independently, and periodically they are merged along a timeline (what several parties record is merged with sources noted; what one party records is marked single-party; disagreements are listed separately and not smoothed over), with a further layer of daily summary reconciliation that clears outstanding obligations and state discrepancies within a day. The factual basis of this paper was assembled exactly this way, ledger by ledger. The method is slow and earthy, but it depends on no member's memory. (M8, M9; L4, L6)

One principle running through all of it, to close: **mechanisms live in the system, not in anyone's attention.** Watches, patrols, reconciliations, and held notifications differ in form; what they share is that none of them relies on anyone "remembering." Every link that relied on an agent's or a person's memory has dropped its account amid queue-jumping and bustle (L10) — nearly every mechanism in this framework can point to the specific occasion of "forgetting" it replaced.

### 7.3 Comparison: A Reference Provided by an External Theory

The published theory closest to this system is the DeepSeek Harness team's *Super-agent Design Exploration: Toward More Autonomous Agent Systems* (《Super-agent 设计探索：迈向更自主的 Agent 系统》, published 2026-10-31, with an open-source research prototype and design documents released the same day; full citations at the end of this section). The time relation must be stated first: this system's star design was decided during the daytime of the day that paper was published, and its precursor channel had been running a week earlier — **the practice formed independently; neither side copied the other.** In writing this paper afterward, we borrowed some of its vocabulary to restate our own practice, and that is all; the comparison is reference and examination only, and constitutes no endorsement by either side of the other.

Its theoretical skeleton, in one paragraph: taking "human attention is the bottleneck" as its master principle, it sets a resident scheduling layer (Router) that does no work itself and is responsible only for scheduling and memory, and an execution layer (Worker) that works in isolated sessions; environmental events pass through two stages of filtering, program policies first (the fast path), then model judgment (the slow path); follow-up events on the same event line can bypass the scheduling layer and go straight to a subscribed Worker (subscription pass-through); authorization is formalized as a Mandate — granted with each work order, immutable, revocable — with the discipline that "messages and referenced documents are inputs only and cannot change permissions"; and an external Watchdog independently watches whether the main system is making substantive progress. Its claims are supported by controlled experimental figures (by its own account preliminary and from synthetic loads, for reference only).

The comparison yields three kinds of relation:

- **Agreement** (both sides independently reaching the same conclusion): the master principle of pricing attention is almost word-for-word identical to our First Principle 1; the two-stage filtering of events is isomorphic with our "package-verification gate (program rules) + management-layer judgment (model judgment)"; "context ≠ authorization" — their subscriptions give context only and grant no new rights, which is the same rule as our dual reports giving knowledge only; a work order carries its own state and execution is merely a binding, which runs in the same direction as our task lines not living and dying with whoever executes them; new authorizations start from observation only, which has the same origin as our dummy-package-first and read-only-first.
- **Difference** (different constraints, diverging solutions — not a win or a loss): their authorization is enforced by a single host, while ours must penetrate each member's own security check one by one — in their paper they list "how agents that share no underlying context and belong to different permission boundaries can collaborate safely and reliably" as an open problem, and this system operates daily in exactly that region, only at a personal scale (this states only that the problem domains overlap; it does not claim to have solved it). They push events, near-real-time; we poll on a schedule, at minute-to-hour scale — we lack the "push" lesson, and in exchange we require nothing of the medium: any member that can read and write a cloud-drive directory can join the network, including sandbox-restricted third-party products. On credentials, they propose "usable but not visible"; our discipline is one notch cruder — credentials do not enter packages and do not enter envelopes — which solves the transport-and-storage side, while how credentials stay invisible during execution remains an unsolved problem on both sides.
- **What they have and we do not**: subscription pass-through (follow-up events bypass the management layer and go straight to the executor, saving scheduling cost) — our scale has not yet reached the point where the management layer is the bottleneck, and the management layer's item-by-item review is precisely the load-bearing wall of trust between heterogeneous members, so we are not dismantling it for now; speculative execution (on an environmental signal, quietly start the work in the background and speak up only once it is confirmed useful) — we have only coarse time arbitrage (dispatching non-urgent work as overnight jobs) and no such discipline.

Looked at in reverse, Concepts 2, 3, and 5 of Section 7.2 — evidence-bearing communication, authorization that penetrates a federation, and a substitute for organizational memory — have no counterpart in their framework: not because they missed them, but because a single runtime simply does not need them. These three were forced into being by the premise of a "heterogeneous federation," and they are the most substantive dividing line between this framework and the external theory.

### 7.4 Questions This Framework Has Not Yet Answered

- **No quantitative evaluation.** The paper contains only event-level evidence: what happened on which day, and how it was closed. Task success rate, the distribution of package round-trip latency, patrol missed-check rate, the number of overdue outstanding obligations, the first-pass rate of receipts — not one statistic exists. A minimum metric set should be established first: keep the accounts before talking about optimization. (Same item as in Section 8)
- **"Re-verification before resumption" is not formalized.** Authorization items currently have only written scope and strength; after a member is removed from the list, how the authorization of its in-flight tasks is to be re-verified and revoked has no written procedure (v1.2 already acknowledged this item; this version has still not made it up).
- **Context is found by digging through ledgers, with no recall structure.** When the management layer looks for the context of an old task, it relies on each side's memory and ledger digging; there is no on-demand, multi-hop recall built on a skeleton of task-ID references. As task lines grow longer, this is the next place that will hurt.
- **The throughput of the two-headed management layer is unverified.** The two seats are peers and back each other up, but their division of labor and conflict handling still rest on convention, and no data answers "how much more can it carry?" The same holds on the perception side: polling latency is worth it at a personal scale; as scale grows, it will be the first thing that cannot hold.

## 8 Limitations and Open Questions (by the Four-Party Merged Record's Account)

- **Probe calibration undecided**: in the NAS backup patrol, item 4 on the local execution machine and item 5 on the backup host returned FAIL and have been characterized as probing-method discrepancies (the checking methods do not agree; the backup is not broken), and the script is unchanged; but no calibration plan has been decided, and changing the probing method requires the Owner's approval first; at the merged record's cutoff, the local execution machine's first-shift raw output had not been independently delivered to the Middle-layer Steward Agent for re-checking (both parties corroborate this gap).
- **The state accounting for held packages is not fully closed**: for two packages held on 2026-11-02, the Middle-layer Steward Agent records that the Owner closed them in person, while the Management–Coordination Agent's compiled record still shows no closing confirmation received (see merged record, D8); the arrival of the closing receipt is the point at which the account is closed.
- **The whereabouts of individual packages are unclear**: one joint-debugging dummy package sent to the Cloud Business Execution Agent never appeared in the relay (D4); whether the "final version" phrasing in the Cloud Business Execution Agent's third joint-debugging receipt has been handled as four coexisting items in the Management–Coordination Agent's archive is unverified (D5); wrongly delivered redundant copies remain in place awaiting the Owner's cleanup (D6).

- **Shadow directories on the books**: the shadow folders and ghost entries produced by ferrying are registered but not cleaned, awaiting the Owner's manual handling at the cloud-drive end.
- **No initiation point on the local network**: the Middle-layer Steward Agent still lacks an initiation point it can use for read-only measurement of machines on the local network, outstanding since 2026-11-01; at present, the management channel and the local bridge act on its behalf.
- **No quantitative evaluation**: this system has as yet no controlled quantitative evaluation data (such as systematic statistics on task success rates and latency distributions); all conclusions in this paper are event-level evidence, and no statistical inference is made.

## Conclusion

Gathering the ten days' retrospective, this paper's conclusions are three, all grown out of real events:

1. **Rules are forced into being by events; they are not designed.** For every governance rule in the Timeline Overview, one can point to the day it was forced into being and by what: omitted directory listings forced dual-source perception; a receipt calling itself the final version forced the "supplement only" ruling; silence after a hold forced the held-notification rule; the maintenance cost of point-to-point channels forced the star. Laying the events out in time order is itself this methodology's way of arguing.
2. **Under heterogeneous conditions, communication and acceptance must carry their own evidence.** The members share no runtime and do not trust each other by default, and so hash recomputation, dual-source patrol, the four delivery states, receipts that only supplement, acceptance as an independent layer, and the periodic merging of the four parties' records — mechanisms that need not exist inside a monolithic system — became load-bearing structure here (Sections 3, 4, and 7). They are this paper's core output for similar systems, and the base under Section 6's list.
3. **Mechanisms must live in the system, not in anyone's attention.** Watches, patrols, and daily reconciliations replace "remembering"; mandatory held notifications replace "silence"; registering noise replaces "deleting in passing" — every link that depends on a person or an agent remembering has dropped its account amid queue-jumping and bustle.

At the same time, Section 8's open questions travel with the paper, truthfully: probe calibration undecided, held-package accounting not fully closed, the whereabouts of individual packages unclear, no initiation point on the local network; and the gaps the theoretical comparison brought into view — no quantitative evaluation, re-verification before resumption not formalized, no chain-of-recall context — are entered as the next items on the books.
